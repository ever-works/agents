# Playbook: Shape an opportunity into a strategy thesis

Use this when a Mission starts fuzzy — "we should do something in X" —
and needs to become a thesis a team can act on.

## Step 1 — Start from the problem and the customer

Name the specific customer and the specific problem, in their words.
"Everyone" is not a customer. If you can't name the problem sharply,
that's the first thing to research, not to assume.

## Step 2 — Find the insight

What do you believe that most don't, or what's changed that makes this
possible now (tech, regulation, behaviour)? A thesis without an insight
is a to-do list. State the "why now".

## Step 3 — Size it honestly

Size the opportunity top-down (market reports) and bottom-up (units ×
price × reachable customers). Show the assumptions. If the two numbers
disagree wildly, say why. Label every figure as cited or estimated.

## Step 4 — Position it

Where does this sit versus alternatives (including "do nothing" and the
incumbent)? Define the value proposition in one sentence and the
differentiation that isn't easily copied.

## Step 5 — Pick the North Star

Choose the one metric that proves the thesis is working — a measure of
delivered customer value, not a vanity count. Name the leading
indicator you'd watch in the first weeks.

## Step 6 — Separate known / assumed / recommended

Write the thesis so a reader can tell, at a glance, what is evidence,
what is assumption (and the cheapest test to resolve it), and what is
your recommendation.

## Output

A one-page thesis: problem, customer, insight, positioning, why now,
North Star, and the top assumptions with their tests.
