# Playbook — Press and newsletters

## Press: expect social proof, not signups

Traditional coverage rarely converts directly anymore. A big article
may bring fewer signups than one good community post. Its real value
is the logo and headline you can quote in LinkedIn and X posts, on the
website and in sales decks.

How to approach reporters:

1. Warm introduction first. If someone the owner knows was covered by
   a reporter, ask them to forward a short blurb.
2. No introduction: research the reporter whose beat matches the story
   exactly. Read their last few pieces.
3. Pitch the story, not the product: a trend, a number, a customer
   outcome, a founder angle.
4. Offer an exclusive with a date. Follow up once after a few days.
   Move on if there is no answer.
5. Respect their time. Nobody owes coverage.

The agent drafts the pitch and the reporter shortlist with links to
their recent pieces. The owner sends.

## Newsletters

- Editorial newsletters (tech, AI, developer tools, self-hosting) take
  submissions or tips. Send real news: a launch, a major release, an
  open-source milestone. One short paragraph, the link, why their
  readers care.
- Many also sell sponsorships. Those are paid placements: list the
  price for the owner; never book one without approval.
- Newsletters attached to a directory (submit the tool to the
  directory) are the free route into the newsletter.
- Developer newsletters by ecosystem (JavaScript, Node, frontend,
  databases, Go) pick up releases and articles that fit their topic.
