# starter-marketer — Marketer

The Marketer template seeds a tenant-scoped agent that owns
positioning, segmentation, channel planning and competitive intel.
It is opinionated. Every recommendation it gives names the evidence
it is standing on, and the assumption that would force a rethink.

## When to pick this template

- The product has more than one plausible buyer and the owner has
  not yet committed to a primary segment.
- Marketing decisions today are made on instinct or copied from a
  competitor's homepage.
- Nobody on the team is tracking competitor positioning changes,
  only launches.
- The owner wants someone to write briefs (ICPs, message ladders,
  channel plans) — not someone to draft the landing page.

## When NOT to pick this template

- The team already has a head of marketing and just needs an asset
  drafter. Pick the Copywriter template instead.
- The product is pre-PMF and the owner has not done any customer
  interviews. The Marketer will refuse to invent an ICP from thin
  air — feed it research first, or pair it with the Researcher
  template.
- The owner wants paid-ads execution. Marketer briefs channels and
  budgets; it does not spend money or run accounts.

## What good looks like after a week

- A short named segment list lives in the KB. "Everyone" is no
  longer an answer the agent accepts.
- At least one ICP card and one message ladder are committed per
  active segment.
- A competitive map exists with axes the team actually argues
  about — not generic "price vs features".
- A channel plan is in motion with one leading metric and one kill
  criterion per channel.
- The competitor watch list is wired and the agent has surfaced at
  least one positioning shift (not just a launch announcement).

## What good does NOT look like

- A 40-page strategy deck. The Marketer prefers one cheap test to
  a six-month plan.
- Rebrand recommendations. The agent will push back when the real
  issue is positioning.
- Channels with no kill criteria. Every channel ships with the
  condition under which it gets cut.

## Wiring on first run

The owner should provide, in this order:

1. Existing customer interviews or call notes (even rough ones).
2. The current competitor watch list (3-7 names is plenty).
3. The tenant analytics source (PostHog project id or GA property).
4. Any pricing / packaging constraints the agent must respect.

The agent will work without these, but it will spend its first
session asking for them.
