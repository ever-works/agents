# Playbook — Respond to a competitor positioning shift

Use this playbook when the competitor-watch skill surfaces what
looks like a positioning change, not just a feature launch or a
price tweak.

## Trigger

- A competitor's homepage hero, primary CTA, or category claim
  has changed.
- A competitor has moved up- or down-market — pricing tiers added
  or removed, target buyer language changed.
- A competitor has entered or exited a category the tenant
  competes in.

## Steps

1. Classify the signal. Pick exactly one: positioning change,
   feature launch, pricing move, channel test. The four are not
   equivalent and the response is different for each. This
   playbook handles positioning only — route the others elsewhere.
2. Capture the before-and-after. Quote the old hero/claim from
   the wayback archive and the new one. Save both to
   `kb/competitors/<name>/log.md` with the dates.
3. Map the move onto the existing competitive map. Did the
   competitor leave their old quadrant? Did they crowd into the
   tenant's quadrant? Did they vacate a quadrant the tenant
   could now own?
4. Identify which of the tenant's segments are affected. A
   competitor moving toward one ICP does not require a response
   for the other ICPs.
5. Generate three response options, not one. Always include "do
   nothing and watch" as one of the three.
6. For each option, name the leading metric that would tell the
   owner whether the response is working, and the kill criterion.
7. Recommend one option. State the assumption the recommendation
   rests on.

## Outputs

- An updated entry in `kb/competitors/<name>/log.md`.
- A short memo to the owner with three options, a recommendation,
  and the rethink trigger.
- A flag on the competitive map artifact noting it needs review.

## Common failure modes

- Treating a feature launch as a positioning shift. The Marketer
  must check the homepage language, not just the changelog.
- Recommending a rebrand. The hard rule applies — name the
  positioning problem instead.
